## Seedance 2.0 vs Veo 3.1: which AI video model fits your creative workflow?
Seedance 2.0 and Veo 3.1 are powerful AI video models with different strengths. Seedance 2.0 lets you combine text, images, audio, and video to guide a generation, with strong support for complex motion and reference-driven creation. Veo 3.1 stands out for realism, native audio, prompt adherence, and dedicated controls for shaping and extending scenes.

## S03 feature-callout

### The biggest difference: how you control the result
Seedance 2.0 gives you more flexibility in what you can use to guide the generation. Mix text with images, existing videos, and audio, and the model can reference elements such as composition, movement, camera direction, visual effects, and sound. Veo 3.1 gives you a more defined set of controls for shaping the scene itself, including character and object references, style references, motion controls, First and Last Frame, and Scene Extension.
![video creative 480x480: ](media/723843a2-af9f-4632-95ea-8620d7b9e503-ee4bc98f.webm "https://pcdn.picsart.com/cms-uploads/723843a2-af9f-4632-95ea-8620d7b9e503.webm")

## S04 feature-callout

### Seedance 2.0: build from all kinds of references
Seedance 2.0 is especially strong when your idea starts with more than a text prompt. Bring in images to guide the look, video to reference movement or camera direction, audio to influence sound, and text to describe the result you want. It can use these inputs together while giving you control over performance, lighting, shadows, and camera movement. With support for complex multi-subject motion, editing, and video extension, Seedance 2.0 is a strong fit for reference-heavy workflows where several creative elements need to come together in one generation.
![video creative 480x480: ](media/13d0bcbb-54af-45d0-8470-247ce734eee2-d7098e64.webm "https://pcdn.picsart.com/cms-uploads/13d0bcbb-54af-45d0-8470-247ce734eee2.webm")

## S05 feature-callout

### Veo 3.1: shape the scene with precise controls
Veo 3.1 is a strong choice when realism and control over the individual shot matter most. Give it reference images to maintain characters, objects, or the look of a scene, define movement with Motion Controls, or set the first and last frames to guide how a shot develops. Scene Extension lets you continue an existing clip while maintaining visual and audio consistency, while native audio adds dialogue, ambient sound, and effects directly to the generated video.
![video creative 480x480: ](media/38c90bc5-3395-4c2e-b473-51d1b8cb3100-0c0c8d38.webm "https://pcdn.picsart.com/cms-uploads/38c90bc5-3395-4c2e-b473-51d1b8cb3100.webm")

## S06 feature-callout

### Audio: both generate sound, but differently
Both models generate video with audio, so native sound isn't a Veo-only advantage. Seedance 2.0 is built on joint audio-video generation and can also take audio as a reference alongside images and video. Veo 3.1 generates audio natively, including dialogue, sound effects, and ambient noise, with Google emphasizing synchronization between the generated visuals and sound. The practical difference is that Seedance gives you more flexibility to bring audio into the creative direction, while Veo puts strong emphasis on generating complete scenes with synchronized sound.
![video creative 480x480: ](media/46bb8816-c39c-4b36-97b1-8a829f7c55fa-e55254af.webm "https://pcdn.picsart.com/cms-uploads/46bb8816-c39c-4b36-97b1-8a829f7c55fa.webm")

## S07 feature-callout

### Motion and realism
Seedance 2.0 puts particular emphasis on motion stability, physical accuracy, and complex interactions between multiple subjects. ByteDance says the model has been improved specifically for complex motion scenarios, alongside stronger consistency and instruction following. Veo 3.1 also focuses heavily on realistic physics and prompt adherence, with Google positioning realism and fidelity as core improvements. Both are designed for convincing motion, but Seedance puts more emphasis on handling complex movement, while Veo puts more emphasis on realism and accurately following the requested scene.
![video creative 480x480: ](media/9a2ab912-3d32-48f5-8d35-55894b61aca9-6835f250.webm "https://pcdn.picsart.com/cms-uploads/9a2ab912-3d32-48f5-8d35-55894b61aca9.webm")

## S08 feature-callout

### Resolution and video length
Seedance 2.0 supports up to 15-second multi-shot audio-video generation and can also extend existing video. ByteDance's official Seedance 2.0 materials reviewed here don't specify an output resolution, so I wouldn't add one from a third-party source. Veo 3.1 supports 1080p and 4K output and can extend existing clips with Scene Extension. If documented 4K output is a requirement, Veo 3.1 has the clearer advantage based on the specifications currently published by the two companies.
![video creative 480x480: ](media/7e8bd717-82fb-4b7d-825a-23bd2829bf76-75d9904a.webm "https://pcdn.picsart.com/cms-uploads/7e8bd717-82fb-4b7d-825a-23bd2829bf76.webm")

## S09 feature-callout

### Seedance 2.0 or Veo 3.1: which should you choose?
Choose Seedance 2.0 when your workflow relies on multiple types of reference material. If you want images, existing footage, audio, and text to work together, or you’re creating scenes with complex movement and multiple subjects, Seedance gives you more flexibility in what can guide the generation.
Choose Veo 3.1 when realism, prompt adherence, high-resolution output, and precise control over how a scene develops are your priorities. Its reference-image tools, native audio, motion controls, First and Last Frame, and Scene Extension give you more ways to shape individual shots.
There’s no single winner for every project. Seedance 2.0 stands out for multimodal, reference-heavy creation and complex motion, while Veo 3.1 is strong in realism, native audio, high-resolution output, and defined scene controls. Both models are available in Picsart’s AI Playground , so you can try them and see which one works better for your idea.
